Irreversibility

There is no undo.

There is no undo and no eraser. To correct, paint over with white or another color, as you would on a canvas. Each artist has to learn the instrument and make it their own.

Gravity & drying

It behaves much like real paint.

Paint runs downward, in whichever direction the iPad is tilted. Hold it upright and the paint runs fast. Lay it flat and it dries where it pools.

The Solvent slider sets the consistency. The Drying speed slider sets how fast it dries. Drying can be slowed, but never stopped or reversed. Thin paint sets in seconds; thick paint takes minutes.

The screen stays flat on purpose: no simulated lighting, gloss or texture. Edges come from the paint itself, drawn cleanly at output resolution.

Pigment & palette

Mixed by light, not by numbers.

Two whites and sixteen digital pigments, each designed by how it absorbs and scatters light, and mixed by those spectra. Color stays spectral until the moment of export. There is no color picker.

There is no black, either. Mix the darkest color you need: Ultramarine Blue with Burnt Umber, or Phthalo Green with Alizarin Crimson.

Everything outside the artboard is the palette. Mix colors and shades there and load them back onto the brush. A color thinned on the palette goes onto the canvas at the same strength.

Paint is used up as you paint. Rub or hold Apple Pencil on a paint well to load the brush; press harder and it fills faster. A new color mixes with what is left on the brush, so wash it first for a clean color. Fingers move and zoom the view. They never paint.

Brushes

A brush is not a stamp. It carries paint.

Each brush works bristle by bristle, holding paint and releasing it. Marks change with pressure, tilt and speed, and with how much paint is left. Raise the Rake level for a rough, scraped texture.

Adjust bristle layout and properties to build brushes of your own, and share them as .mimbrush files. A loaded brush can hang on the brush rack, paint and all, and be picked up again later.

Two color modes

One for paper. One for light.

Each artwork chooses its mode when it is created. Both mix the same way, and both turn muddy if you overmix.

Pixel Art mode

The flow of paint, on a pixel grid.

Pixel Art mode keeps every bit of the physics. Paint still runs, merges and dries. Only the picture is read out as pixels, from 16 to 512 across.

Limit the color steps and the paint snaps to a reduced palette. At four steps, every pixel lands on one of 64 fixed colors.

Output

Made with the print as the reference.

In Solid mode the artwork is measured in millimeters: paper A and B sizes, photo prints, F-size canvases and more. The size is fixed when the artwork is created and cannot change afterward.

Privacy

No accounts, no advertising, no tracking. Everything is processed on the device, and an artwork leaves it only when you export it.
