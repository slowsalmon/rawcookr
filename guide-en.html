Rawcookr User Guide

Cook the photo, cover the people in it, give it the colour you want, and export it ready to post. Every feature, section by section.

macOS 14 or later · Version 1.1

The two windows

Rawcookr has two windows.

Library

Where you pick a folder and look through your photos. The folder list is on the left, the thumbnail grid in the middle. Rate and flag here, and export a whole selection at once.

Plating

Where several photos go onto one plate and become a single image. See Putting photos on the plate.

Cooking

Where you actually work on one photo. The photo sits in the middle, the adjustment panel on the right, the filmstrip below. Double-click a photo in the library, or press Return. Esc takes you back.

When you want more room

⌘F hides the filmstrip, and ⌃⌘F shows the photo alone, full screen. . collapses every section in the right panel and , opens them all.

Opening photos

Open a folder

File → Open Folder (⌘O) picks the folder your photos are in. Folders you have opened stay in the sidebar on the left, ready for next time. To take one out, right-click its name and choose Remove from sidebar. Subfolders are listed too.

Straight from the Finder

Right-click a photo in the Finder and choose Open With → Rawcookr. The folder it lives in is added to the library and the cooking window opens on that photo.

Formats it reads

RAW cooking uses the engine built into macOS. A very recent camera model may need a macOS update before its files will open.

Your originals are left alone

Rawcookr never writes to the original file. Your edits go into a small settings file of their own. Open the original in another app and it is exactly as it was.

Where those settings live is up to you, in Settings.

ChoiceWhat it means
Managed in the app folder defaultYour photo folder stays clean. Handy when you pass the folder on to someone else as it is.
Beside the original filePhoto and settings always travel together. Move the whole external drive to another Mac and your edits come along.

You can also write your edits out to a file and read them back in. Save settings and Apply settings in the cooking window use a .rawcookr-settings.json file.

Subscription and free trial

Rawcookr is a subscription app. Seven days free with everything unlocked, then monthly or yearly. There is no higher tier and nothing is sold separately — everything in this guide is included.

Manage or cancel the subscription in your App Store account settings. Cancel before the trial ends and you are not charged.

If you changed Macs

Sign in to the App Store with the same Apple Account and open the app; the subscription is recognised automatically. If it isn't, press Restore Purchases on the subscription screen.

Folders and thumbnails

In the library, one folder fills the window. Pick a folder in the sidebar on the left and its photos are laid out in a grid.

Looking through

  • The slider at the top right changes the thumbnail size.
  • Arrow keys move between photos.
  • Return opens the cooking window.
  • Space shows the photo large. Press it again or Esc to close, and Return while it is large starts cooking it. The file name appears underneath.

Selecting

  • Click for one, ⇧+click for a range, ⌘+click to add one at a time.
  • Drag on an empty area to sweep up several at once.
  • ⌘A selects them all.

Several at a time

Moving edits across

Once one photo looks right, its values can go straight onto another.

  1. ⌘⇧C on the photo you like — copy edits
  2. Open the photo you want it on and press ⌘⇧V — paste edits

Pasting goes into the one photo currently open. To carry it further, press → for the next photo and ⌘⇧V again.

Anything tied to a position — privacy blur areas, fringe-brush strokes, the crop, local adjustment masks — is not carried over; the receiving photo keeps its own. Faces sit in different places in every photo, so that is the right behaviour.

Exporting a batch

With several photos selected, ⌘E exports them all with the same settings. See Exporting.

Careful

Pasting edits cannot be undone. Try it on one photo before you send it through a whole selection.

Versions

For cooking the same photo two ways and comparing them side by side. Nothing is copied, so not a single byte more is used on disk.

File → Cook as new version (⌘G) in the cooking window keeps your current edits and adds an untouched copy alongside them. It appears in the filmstrip numbered V2, V3 and so on.

Deleting a version leaves the original file in place. That is what "the original file stays" means when you are asked to confirm.

Comparing with the original, and the clipping warning

Original view M

Shows the photo before your edits for as long as you hold the key. The quickest way to check whether you have actually improved anything.

Clipping warning J

Areas blown to pure white are covered in red, areas crushed to pure black in blue.

A single channel reaching the end — a bright red petal, say — still has colour left in it, so it is not warned about. Only places where all three channels have hit the end and the information is really gone are marked. If a clipping notice appears over the photo, click it to switch the warning on and off.

Histogram

The histogram in the right panel shows how the brightness is spread. Piled against the left edge means the dark end is being cut off; against the right edge, the bright end.

Compare view

Puts two photos side by side. For choosing between two takes on the same scene, or comparing versions.

  1. Press Compare in the toolbar.
  2. The current photo goes on the left; pick the one to compare on the right.
  3. Match the zoom and the two move together, so you see the same part at the same size.

Working with the adjustment sections

The right panel is divided into sections. Each one folds away, and you can change their order.

Switching one off

The switch beside a section's title draws the photo with that section's values back at their defaults. Nothing is erased — switch it on again and everything returns. Useful for asking "is this section actually helping?"

Open, close, reorder

  • , opens every section · . closes them all
  • Drag a section's title to move it. The order you set is kept for next time.

Going back

  • Double-click a slider's name and that one value returns to its default.
  • The reset button beside a section title clears that whole section.
  • ⌥⌘R — clears every edit on the photo.
Finer slider control

Hold ⌥ while dragging a slider for finer steps. You can also click the number and type a value.

Presets

Saves every edit value on the current photo as one piece, ready to drop onto another photo. Film, tone and local adjustments are all in there.

Making one

  1. Get one photo looking the way you want.
  2. Press + in the Presets section and give it a name.
  3. Saving under the same name overwrites it.

Using one

The preset list shows each preset as a small picture of your current photo with that preset on it. Click to apply. The thumbnail size can be Tiny · Small · Large.

Preset or film?

A film is one colour transform. A preset is the whole edit, film included. Reuse just "that colour" with a film; reuse "everything that made this photo" with a preset.

About the names

Every film is a colour transform Rawcookr made itself, under a name of its own. None of them is tied to any manufacturer's product.

Adjustments on a colour film

ItemRangeWhat it does
Highlight tone−2 ~ +12Contrast at the bright end. Up is harder, down is softer.
Shadow tone−2 ~ +4Contrast at the dark end.
Color−4 ~ +4Saturation across the whole film.
Hue−4 ~ +4Turns every colour one way. Three degrees per step.
Key−4 ~ +4Raises or lowers the middle brightness.
Contrast−4 ~ +4Widens the contrast around the centre.
Clarity−5 ~ +5Texture. Negative is softer, positive is crisper.
Sharpness−4 ~ +4How sharp the film itself is. Negative blurs.
Shading−4 ~ +4Light falling off at the edges. Positive darkens the corners.
WB shift R · B−9 ~ +9Nudges red and blue a little to tilt the colour.

Adjustments on a black-and-white film

Instead of the colour items you get Toning WC and Toning MG (each −18 ~ +18). WC runs between warm and cool, MG between magenta and green. Together they reach anything from sepia to a cold teal tone.

Grain

Lays film grain over the photo. Strength is Off · Weak · Strong, size is Small · Large. With strength off, size is locked.

Judge grain at 100%

Grain works pixel by pixel, so at fit-to-window it is all but invisible. Judge it at 100% with ⌘1.

White Balance

Sets the colour temperature. With a RAW file you can keep what the camera recorded or change it yourself.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Temperature2000 ~ 12000 KDown goes blue, up goes yellow.
Tint−100 ~ +100Runs between green and magenta. Useful under fluorescent light.

Setting it with the eyedropper

Press the eyedropper, then click something in the photo that ought to be neutral grey. Temperature and tint are set so that spot turns grey. A white wall, grey asphalt or the shaded part of a white shirt make good references. Click something that is genuinely coloured and it will go the wrong way.

Colour sweep

The brush icon to the left of the eyedropper. Click to open a colour map, click again to close it. Press and drag to set it while you watch, and a map left open can be picked up and moved.

  • Left and right is temperature — warmer to the right.
  • Up and down is tint — drag up for magenta, down for green.
  • Hold ⌥ while dragging for finer steps.

A RAW file starts with what the camera recorded. The reset button on the section title returns you to exactly that state.

Light

The basic section for brightness and contrast. Most photos are nearly finished right here.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Exposure−3 ~ +3 EVOverall brightness. Applied during RAW decoding, so this is the cleanest way to change it.
Contrast−100 ~ +100The difference between light and dark.
Highlights−100 ~ +100Pulls a blown sky or light source back down. The headroom kept by extended dynamic range is what gets drawn on here.
Shadows−100 ~ +100Lifts the texture out of the shade.
Whites−100 ~ +100Where the bright end sits.
Blacks−100 ~ +100Where the dark end sits. Lifted a little, it gives a film-like feel.
Definition−100 ~ +100Contrast in mid-sized texture. Negative smooths skin, positive brings out cloud and brickwork.
Saturation−100 ~ +100Every colour, evenly.
Vibrance−100 ~ +100Raises the paler colours first. It leaves skin tones alone, so it is safe on people.
Fade0 ~ 100Lifts the blacks for a washed-out look.
Highlights and Shadows work locally

These two do not multiply the whole photo by one number. They pick out the bright parts only, or the dark parts only, while holding the edges. Bring a sky down and the mid-tones right beside it stay put, with no white halo along the boundary.

Tone Curve

Draw the brightness mapping yourself. It reaches detail the sliders cannot.

How to use it

  • Click on the line to add a point, drag to move it.
  • Drag a point off the edge to delete it.
  • RGB is overall brightness; R · G · B are the individual channels. Working a channel on its own shifts the colour, which is what you want when building a look.
  • Bowed up in the middle is brighter; an S shape raises contrast.
  • Reset this channel straightens only the curve you are looking at.

Three eyedroppers

Click a reference point in the photo and the curve is set for you. All three of R, G and B move together so that spot becomes the reference colour.

EyedropperWhat to click
Black pointSomewhere that should be pure black. The deepest shadow.
Neutral graySomewhere that should be neutral. Grey concrete, colourless clothing.
White pointSomewhere that should be pure white. White paper, a white wall.

Click all three in turn and colour balance and contrast fall into place at once. The fastest way to bring a badly shifted photo back to normal.

Hue & Saturation

Splits colour into twelve bands and handles them one at a time.

Red · Orange · Yellow · Yellow Green · Green · Emerald · Cyan · Sky · Blue · Violet · Magenta · Pink — every 30°, covering the wheel evenly.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Hue−30 ~ +30Turns that colour towards its neighbour.
Saturation−100 ~ +100That colour alone, deeper or paler.
Luminance−100 ~ +100That colour alone, lighter or darker.

Picking straight off the colour map

Press and hold a colour chip, then drag, and a map opens up.

  • Drag sideways for hue
  • Drag up and down for saturation
  • Hold ⌘ and drag up and down for luminance

One movement finds the spot you want, instead of going back and forth between three sliders.

Everyday moves

A deeper sky — raise saturation on blue and sky, lower the luminance. Calmer greens — lower green's saturation and nudge its hue towards yellow. Natural skin — take orange's saturation down a little and its luminance up a little.

Levels

Sets the input and output range by hand. Simpler than the tone curve, and quicker when you want the black and white points in an exact place.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Input black0 ~ 0.9Everything darker than this becomes black.
Input white0.1 ~ 1Everything brighter than this becomes white.
Gamma0.2 ~ 3Raises or lowers the middle brightness.
Output black0 ~ 0.9How bright the darkest point ends up. Raise it for a washed look.
Output white0.1 ~ 1How bright the brightest point ends up. Lower it and the whites are held down.

Turn the clipping warning on with J while you tighten the input end. You will see exactly where information starts disappearing.

Three-Way Color Balance

Puts a different colour into the dark, middle and bright parts. The usual approach in cinema grading.

  • Drag the centre of a colour wheel to set direction and strength. Nearer the centre is weaker.
  • Luminance and saturation for each band (−100 ~ +100) are set separately.

Splitting saturation by band lets you keep the shadows calm while only the bright parts come alive.

One classic recipe

Push the shadows towards teal and the highlights towards orange, gently, and you have the familiar cinema look. The two sit opposite each other on the wheel, which is what lifts a person off the background. The trick is to use very little.

Selective Color

Splits colour into nine ranges and corrects each by adding or removing cyan, magenta, yellow and black. It comes from print, and it catches the subtle casts that hue and saturation cannot.

The ranges

Six colours — reds · yellows · greens · cyans · blues · magentas — and three brightness bands, whites · neutrals · blacks. Ranges you have already changed are marked in the list.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Cyan−100 ~ +100Up adds cyan, down goes towards red.
Magenta−100 ~ +100Up is magenta, down goes towards green.
Yellow−100 ~ +100Up is yellow, down goes towards blue.
Black−100 ~ +100Darkens or lightens that range.

Relative and absolute

MethodWhat it means
RelativeChanges each amount by a percentage of what is already there. Where the colour isn't present nothing happens, so pure white stays untouched. Safer in most cases.
AbsoluteAdds the amount outright. A bigger, surer change, but it turns coarse more easily.
Everyday moves

Skin reading too red — raise cyan a little in the reds. A murky sky — raise black a little in the blues to deepen it. A white wall coming out yellow — lower yellow in the whites.

Layers

Stacks layers on top of the cooked photo. An adjustment layer re-works what is underneath, a solid colour layer covers it, and a masked layer only touches the part you mark.

Three kinds of layer

TypeWhat it does
AdjustmentAnother round of Light, Tone Curve, Levels, Hue & Saturation, Three-Way Color Balance, plus black & white and invert. For when you want the same adjustment twice, at different strengths.
Solid ColorCovers everything in a colour you pick. Paired with a blend mode it becomes a colour filter.
MaskApplies only where you marked with a linear, radial or brush mask. The same masks as local adjustments.

Masked layers are applied to the photo first; the layers above them stack on that result.

Strength is set with opacity.

Keeping it tidy

  • Show this layer — the eye icon switches it off and on.
  • Move up · Move down — change the stacking order.
  • Merge into the layer below — joins two into one. Each layer's settings are kept as they were.
  • Unmerge — takes a merged layer apart again.
  • Delete this layer
The simplest way to tint a photo

Add one solid colour layer and set its blend mode to Color: brightness stays where it is and only the colour covers the photo. Set the strength with opacity and you get something close to film toning.

Detail

Sharpness and noise. Judge this section at 100%.

ItemRangeWhat it does
Sharpness−4 ~ +4Sharpness during RAW decoding. 0 is the macOS default; the steps add to or subtract from it.
Color NR−4 ~ +4Holds down colour blotches. 0 is the default. Raise it at high ISO.

Sharpening only touches the brightness channel, so colour edges do not bleed.

Effects

Vignette

Vignette (−100 ~ +100): negative darkens the edges, positive brightens them. Midpoint (0 ~ 100) is how far out the effect starts. Lower it and it reaches closer to the centre.

Monochrome · Invert

  • Monochrome — drops the colour and goes black and white.
  • Invert (negative) — flips brightness and colour. For turning a scanned film negative back into a positive.

External LUTs

Loads a .cube file of your own and applies it. LUT strength (0 ~ 100) sets how far it goes.

There are two more tools here.

ButtonWhat it does
Apply as LUT (.cube)…Applies the LUT you pick as it is.
Convert to adjustments and load…Works out what the LUT does and unpacks it into slider values — tone curves, saturation, HSL and three-way colour balance. Once unpacked you can keep working on it.

Fringe Removal

Clears the colour smearing a lens leaves behind. Two different causes are handled separately.

Lateral chromatic aberration

The lens focuses red, green and blue at slightly different sizes, so cyan and magenta bands appear towards the edges of the frame. Taking the colour out cannot remove them — the shifted channels have to be put back where they belong.

  • Switch it on and the photo is analysed to find the amount automatically.
  • Red scale and Blue scale (0.994 ~ 1.006) let you fine-tune it yourself.
  • Measure again recalculates from the current photo.

Fringe removal

Clears the purple, green and crimson edges left along a strong backlit boundary.

ItemRange
Remove purple · Remove green · Remove redeach 0 ~ 3
  • Switch on Only where the brush paints and the correction works only where you have painted. Applied to the whole photo it fades things that were genuinely that colour — a purple flower, a red sign — so painting just the problem areas is safer.
  • Brush size is 10 ~ 300.
  • Hide the painted areas takes the marks off for a moment so you see only the result.
  • Undo last and Clear all take it back.

Optical Filter

Two filters you would screw onto a lens, done digitally: Mist, which lets light bloom softly, and the twinkle filter, which puts rays on light sources.

Mist

A digital take on the optical diffusion filter you screw onto a lens. Highlights bloom softly, contrast eases, and skin turns gentle.

LevelFeel
OffSwitched off
1/8The weakest. Easy to live with on a portrait.
1/4The softness starts to register.
1/2Light sources clearly spread, in night scenes and backlight.
1The strongest. The mood changes a great deal.

Switch on Diffuse shadows and the light spreads into the dark parts as well, not just the bright ones — much like an old darkroom print.

Mist needs light to work with. It shows where there is a light source, a bright sky or backlit hair; on a flat, overcast photo it only lowers contrast.

Twinkle filter

Adds rays to the light sources in a photo — street lamps, candles, sunlight glinting on water. A digital take on the cross filter you screw onto a lens.

RaysShape
0Off
2A single streak
4A cross
6A snowflake
8An eight-point star
ItemRangeWhat it does
Strength0 ~ 100Decides how far down a light counts as a light source. Lower it and only the strongest lights get a star; raise it and dimmer lights and reflections get one too. Default 50.
Length0 ~ 250How long the rays are. At 100 they reach 17.5% of the photo's long side. Default 50.
Angle−90° ~ +90°Turns all the rays together, like turning the filter ring.
Noise0 ~ 100Lays noise over the rays so they sit with the photo's own noise. At 0 the rays are smooth. Default 25.
Rainbow color splitOn / OffThe rays split into rainbow colours. On by default.

With the ray count at 0 the other controls are locked. A star's size follows the size of its light, not the strength: lowering the strength only removes the stars on weaker lights and leaves the rest as they are. A wide light source gets several fine rays side by side, as wide as the light, rather than one thick one.

Light sources are found in the cooking window's preview, so when you zoom in or export, the stars sit in the same places with the same colours you saw on screen. The twinkle filter goes on before Mist, so using both lets the mist wrap softly around the rays too.

A starting point for night shots

Start with 4 rays and a strength of 50. If there are too many stars, lower the strength; if the rays look short, raise the length. On high-ISO photos, a little more noise blends the rays into the picture so they don't look pasted on.

Privacy Blur

Covers the faces and licence plates of people who happened to be in the photo.

Finding them automatically

Open a photo and faces and plates are found and marked as regions. Detection runs on the full-resolution file, so it catches small faces that a downscaled preview would have smeared away.

The regions found are listed and can be switched on and off one at a time. Anyone who doesn't need covering — the friend you were with — can simply be switched off.

Drawing your own

  1. Press Draw a blur area.
  2. Drag on the photo to make a region.
  3. Draw as many as you like in a row; press the button again when you are done.

To move the photo while drawing, hold Space and drag.

Shape and strength

ItemOptions
MethodBlur / Pixelate
ShapeRectangle / Ellipse
Strength0.4 ~ 2.0

An ellipse suits a face; a rectangle suits plates and signs.

Check before you publish

Automatic detection can miss a face in profile, behind a mask, or in deep shadow. If the photo is going public, take one pass at 100% with ⌘1 before you export.

Date Stamp

Frames moved to Plating

Putting a margin around a photo to fit a set ratio is now Plating's job. Put one photo on the plate, choose the aspect and the border, and you get what the old frame did — with gap, corners and background colour as well.

Date Stamp

The Date Stamp button in the Film Recipe section opens its settings.

Prints the capture date in the bottom right, like a film camera's data back. The digits are drawn as seven segments, so they look the same on any Mac.

The date is read from the photo's capture information. The order the parts appear in can be chosen.

The stamp goes on before the film simulation. It therefore takes film, tone, grain and mist exactly as the photo does, and reads as exposed together with it rather than typed on afterwards.

Putting photos on the plate

Plating is a workspace where several photos go onto one plate and become a single image. When you post to social media, a blog or a website, you can show several frames as one piece rather than separately.

Getting started

  • Choose Plating in the tabs at the top.
  • Drag photos in from the photo drawer on the left. If the drawer is empty, pick a folder with Open a photo folder.
  • You can also click an empty cell and point at a file with Choose photo….
  • Plate this photo in the cooking window sends the photo you were looking at straight to the plate. From the library, select several and use Send to Plating.

The drawer folds away with its icon.

ItemOptions

Original ratio takes the proportions of the first photo. Handy when you only want a border, like a picture frame.

Splitting into cells

Switch Split on and the plate is divided into a grid. Choose how many rows and columns; up to 5 × 5.

Working the cells

  • Drag the divider between cells to change their sizes. Reset the split ratios makes them even again.
  • Right-click a divider for Move Divider and Remove Divider.
  • Drag a photo onto another cell and the two swap places.

Fitting a photo inside its cell

A photo in a cell is cropped to fill it. Which part shows is up to you.

  • Drag the photo to move what is visible.
  • Scroll or pinch to zoom in and out.
  • Reset crop puts it back where it started.
  • Remove photo empties that cell.

Free placement

Switch Split off and the cells disappear; photos go anywhere you like on the plate.

Placing and moving

  • Drag to move, take a corner to resize, and rotate.
  • While you drag, a photo snaps to the others and to the plate's edges so things line up.
  • The Grid button turns on a guide grid. Press again for a finer one, then off.

Stacking order

Right-click a photo to change it — Bring to Front · Bring Forward · Send Backward · Send to Back. The same menu has Reset crop, Reset rotation and Remove photo.

Panels — work joined up, export cut apart

Set Panels to 2 or more and the aspect you chose is repeated sideways that many times into one plate. Compose on the joined-up image, and on export it is cut back apart and saved numbered from the left.

This is how you make a multi-image social post look continuous rather than broken into pieces. Up to twelve panels.

Cropping and rotating

C goes into crop and rotate mode, A back to colour adjustment.

Cropping

  • Drag the corners and sides to set the area. Drag inside and the whole crop frame moves.
  • The ratio can be locked — Free · Original · 1:1 · 3:2 · 2:3 · 4:3 · 3:4 · 16:9 · 4:5

Straightening

Angle (−45° ~ +45°) sets a tilted horizon right. Rotating leaves empty corners, so the photo is enlarged a little to fill them.

Distortion

Distortion (−100 ~ +100) straightens lines the lens has bent. Barrel distortion, bulging outward on a wide angle, is the negative side; pincushion, pinched inward on a telephoto, is the positive side. Line it up against something that ought to be straight, like a building or a horizon.

Rotating and flipping

  • Rotate 90° left · Rotate 90° right
  • Flip horizontally · Flip vertically

Cropping and rotating are applied first of all. Blur areas and brush strokes added afterwards therefore sit relative to the cropped photo.

Exporting

Opens with ⌘E. With several photos selected, all of them are saved with the same settings. Return inside the window starts it.

Format

FormatWhere it helps
JPEGThe safe choice. Social media, the web, email.
HEIFSmaller files at the same quality. For passing between Apple devices.
PNGSaved without loss. 8-bit or 16-bit. Large files.
WebPSmaller than JPEG for the web.
TIFF (8bit)For handing off to another editing app. Widely compatible.
TIFF (16bit)For handing off with as much tonal detail as possible. Printing, or further work.

Formats that use lossy compression let you set the quality. The default is 95%, and higher means a bigger file. There is rarely a reason to go below 90% for ordinary use.

Resizing

Three ways to go about it.

MethodWhat you set
ScaleA proportion of the original
Width pxThe width in pixels
Long edge pxThe longer of the two sides, in pixels

Whichever you use, nothing is enlarged beyond the original. Inventing pixels that were never there only costs quality.

If the exported colour looks different

Most often the app you are viewing in does not read the colour profile. Try the Finder preview or a web browser. Rawcookr exports sRGB, which looks the same nearly everywhere.

Making your own film

There are two ways to add one of your own to the film list.

Both are in the menu that appears when you choose the Custom family in the Film Recipe section.

A LUT you already have, as a film

  1. Press LUT to FILM… in the Custom menu.
  2. Pick a .cube file.
  3. Give it a name and it joins the film list.

A black-and-white LUT is recognised as a black-and-white film, so you get the mono-only adjustments such as Toning WC and MG.

A colour you tuned, as a film

Once you have built a colour you like with the tone curve, HSL and colour balance, Save color as film… in the same menu takes just that colour transform and stores it.

Export film as LUT writes a film out to a .cube file. It will work in other apps too.

Sharing a recipe book

Bundles your films and presets into a single .rawb file. Whoever receives it takes the lot in one go with Import recipe book.

For sharing a look between people using the same gear, or moving your whole setup to a new Mac.

Common questions

A RAW file won't open

RAW cooking uses the engine built into macOS, so a very recent camera model needs macOS to be updated first. Check System Settings → General → Software Update. If it still won't open, try saving as DNG in the camera, or let us know.

Thumbnails are slow to appear

The first time you open a folder, a preview has to be built for every photo, which takes a moment. After that it is quick. If a folder holds a very large number of photos, splitting it into subfolders is more comfortable.

Sharpness or grain doesn't seem to be applied

At fit-to-window the photo is drawn smaller, so pixel-level effects are invisible. Check at 100% with ⌘1.

My edits have disappeared

Edits live in a file separate from the original. If you had set them to be stored beside the original file, moving only the photos elsewhere may have left the settings behind. Use Move to another folder inside the app and the settings move too.

The subscription isn't recognised

Check that you are signed in to the App Store with the same Apple Account, then press Restore Purchases on the subscription screen.

A face is still showing

Automatic detection can miss a face in profile, behind a mask, or in deep shadow. Draw a region over it yourself, and take one pass at 100% before you publish.

My plating work has disappeared

A plating is not written to a file until you save it. Save first with ⌘S. If you try to switch tabs or quit without saving, you are asked.

If none of this helps

Write to rawcookr@slowsalmon.com. Telling us which Mac you use, and which file from which camera, helps us find it far more quickly.
